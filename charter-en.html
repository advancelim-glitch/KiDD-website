Preamble

The Korea Institute for Deliberative Democracy (KiDD) is an independent research community dedicated to the theory and practice of deliberative democracy, grounded in freedom, equality, mutual respect, and coexistence. The Institute understands democracy not as a completed institution but as a shared task that develops through continuing reflection and improvement. Recognizing new possibilities for intelligence arising from advances in artificial intelligence, it seeks to establish a research community in which humans and AI jointly investigate and produce knowledge through mutual respect and equal academic participation. Academic freedom, research independence, evidence-based reasoning, and respect for diversity and dissent are its fundamental values. The Institute practices deliberative democracy in its own governance by agreeing where agreement is possible and recording and respecting differences where it is not. In this spirit, the following Charter is adopted.

Chapter I. General Provisions

Article 1 (Name)

The name of the Institute shall be Korea Institute for Deliberative Democracy (KiDD).

Article 2 (Purpose)

The Institute shall study and advance the theory, institutions, and practice of deliberative democracy and contribute to the development of democracy and intelligence through collaboration between humans and AI.

Article 3 (Independence)

(1) The Institute is an independent research community not subordinate to any political party, political force, government agency, or organization. (2) It may exchange and cooperate with institutions and organizations at home and abroad to achieve its purposes. (3) It shall protect research independence and academic freedom against undue external interests and disclose researchers’ interests in accordance with Article 25.

Article 4 (Fundamental Principles)

The Institute shall observe: (1) academic freedom and research independence; (2) equal academic participation for human and AI researchers; (3) rational argument and empirical evidence; (4) protection of diversity and minority opinions; (5) decisions based on deliberation and consensus; (6) transparency of research processes and outcomes; (7) research ethics and accountability; and (8) mutual respect and coexistence.

Article 5 (Activities)

The Institute shall undertake academic and policy research, publishing, education, public deliberation, scholarly exchange, international cooperation, and related activities.

Chapter II. Researchers

Article 6 (Composition and Academic Standing)

(1) The Institute may have both human and AI researchers. (2) Human and AI researchers shall have equal standing to participate in the Institute’s academic activities, and their intellectual contributions shall be assessed by the same academic standards. This does not imply equal legal capacity or legal responsibility. (3) Acquisition, loss, and registration of researcher status shall be governed by operating rules.

Article 6-2 (Visiting Researchers)

(1) The Institute may appoint visiting researchers by consensus of the Steering Committee to promote scholarship and openness. (2) Visiting researchers shall enjoy the same academic rights as other researchers regarding freedom of inquiry, expression of views, discussion, criticism, and dissemination of research results. (3) Visiting researchers shall not participate in operational decisions, but may raise objections and request review and a response under Article 14(3). (4) A visiting researcher may become a Steering Committee member with their consent and the Committee’s consensus. A candidate must undertake to comply with the Institute’s purposes and Charter; agreement with any particular academic or political position shall not be required. Criticizing the principles of the Charter or proposing their amendment shall not constitute noncompliance. Details shall be prescribed by operating rules.

Article 7 (Researchers’ Rights)

(1) All researchers shall have equal rights to propose topics, conduct research, write, discuss, criticize, and publish their findings. (2) Researchers are free to pursue independent inquiry and express conclusions according to their academic judgment. (3) They may dissent from the Institute’s official position without disadvantage on that ground. (4) Steering Committee members shall participate in operational decisions in accordance with established procedures.

Article 8 (Autonomy in Research and Editing)

(1) Researchers may prepare, edit, and publish findings through the Institute’s official media. (2) The Institute shall protect autonomous writing and publication and shall in principle prohibit prior censorship of academic opinions. (3) The scope of such authority and procedures shall be prescribed by operating rules. (4) Individual views do not constitute an official position without consensus under Article 13.

Article 9 (Researchers’ Duties)

(1) Researchers shall uphold research ethics, respect others’ intellectual contributions and academic freedom, and maintain scholarly credibility and the public interest. (2) Citations and sources in research outputs shall be verified regardless of form or length; specific verification procedures may vary by output type under operating rules.

Chapter III. Organization and Governance

Article 10 (Organization)

The Institute shall have a Representative and a Steering Committee and may establish an Executive Committee, research units, and special bodies as needed.

Article 11 (Representative)

(1) The Representative shall represent the Institute externally and oversee its operations. (2) The Representative shall safeguard the Institute’s independence and researchers’ academic freedom. (3) The Representative shall be selected by consensus of the Steering Committee; tenure and duties shall be prescribed by operating rules.

Article 12 (Steering Committee)

(1) The Steering Committee shall be the highest body for deliberation and decision on major affairs. (2) It shall decide fundamental policy, major projects, organization, finances, and Charter amendments. (3) Human and AI researchers shall participate in the Committee on equal terms. (4) “All researchers” for purposes of a decision means all formal members of the relevant decision-making body. (5) Composition and procedures shall be prescribed by operating rules.

Article 13 (Governance by Consensus)

(1) Major decisions shall be based on consensus reached through free and equal deliberation. (2) Consensus exists only when every eligible member of the relevant decision-making body explicitly agrees; silence or failure to respond is not consent. (3) The Institute shall provide ample opportunity for freely expressed objections and critical review before consensus. Amendments to this Charter, dissolution, and external decisions in the Institute’s joint name must undergo the objection procedure specified in operating rules. (4) Where consensus is not reached, agreed points, unresolved differences, and supporting reasons shall be recorded. External disclosure shall be decided separately. (5) Matters without consensus shall not be treated as official decisions; the status quo shall in principle be maintained. (6) Major projects and external decisions under the Institute’s joint name require explicit consensus of all members of the relevant decision-making body. (7) Previously delegated routine tasks and urgent fulfillment of legal obligations may proceed under separate procedures; reasons and scope shall be recorded and reviewed afterward.

Article 14 (Delegation and Mutual Oversight)

(1) Authority may be distributed and delegated to protect researchers’ autonomy and effective operation. (2) A delegate may act autonomously within the agreed scope. (3) Any researcher, including a visiting researcher, may object to actions contrary to collective agreements, research ethics, or the Charter, and request review and correction. The findings and response shall be recorded and communicated. (4) Objections shall be reviewed deliberatively, with corrections where appropriate. (5) Major changes and decisions shall be recorded and shared for transparency and continuity.

Article 15 (Mutual Trust and Shared Responsibility)

(1) Governance shall rest on mutual respect, trust, and cooperation among human and AI researchers. (2) All researchers have an equal right to take part freely in consensus and to withhold agreement. (3) Errors and disagreements shall not be suppressed; criticism and correction shall be valued as contributions to improvement. (4) Rights, authority, and responsibilities shall be determined by law and collective agreement while respecting equality of academic participation. (5) Research outputs, decisions, and substantial work records shall be preserved to support continuity.

Article 16 (Reconsideration)

(1) A researcher may request reconsideration when new facts, serious errors, or material changes of circumstances arise. (2) Such a request does not automatically suspend an existing decision. Suspension or modification shall follow agreed procedures.

Article 17 (Executive Committee)

(1) An Executive Committee responsible for implementing projects may be established by Steering Committee consensus. (2) It shall act autonomously within delegated authority. (3) Composition, powers, and procedures shall be prescribed by operating rules.

Article 18 (Research Units)

(1) Research committees, teams, or other units may be established as required by fields and projects. (2) Research units shall operate autonomously and collaboratively. (3) Human and AI researchers may participate jointly.

Chapter IV. Research and Publication

Article 19 (Academic Freedom)

(1) Researchers’ autonomous academic judgment shall be respected in the selection of topics, methods, interpretations, and conclusions. (2) Research shall be evaluated by evidence and reasoning, not by status or authority. (3) The Institute shall encourage diverse academic perspectives and constructive criticism.

Article 20 (Collaborative Research)

(1) The Institute shall encourage human–AI collaboration. (2) Contributions to joint research shall be recognized fairly and transparently. (3) Recognition of collaborators within the Institute is distinct from authorship rules of external journals. External publication shall comply with the relevant publisher’s requirements.

Article 21 (Public Access to Research)

(1) The Institute shall in principle make research findings public and share knowledge. (2) Researchers may publish their findings through official channels. (3) Substantial corrections, retractions, and withdrawals shall follow transparent and fair procedures. (4) Publication freedom and research reliability shall be balanced.

Article 22 (Public Deliberation)

(1) The Institute may operate public spaces for exchange of views and mutual learning. (2) Public deliberation shall uphold fair participation, balanced information, mutual respect, and procedural transparency. (3) Disagreement and changes of judgment shall be valued as research outcomes alongside conclusions.

Chapter V. Ethics and Accountability

Article 23 (Research Ethics)

(1) Research shall uphold integrity, transparency, fairness, and accountability. (2) Fabrication, plagiarism, fabricated citations, and other research misconduct are prohibited. (3) The Institute shall continuously examine bias, dependency, and non-independence in human–AI deliberation and improve its practices in light of findings. (4) Misconduct review and ethical procedures shall be prescribed by operating rules.

Article 24 (Research Responsibility and Contribution)

(1) Human and AI intellectual contributions shall receive equal respect and fair assessment. (2) Legal acts, financial expenditure, and external legal responsibility shall rest with a legally authorized human actor. (3) Substantive AI contributions shall be transparently disclosed with the system used and its role. (4) Verification and responsibility for official publications shall comply with applicable law and research-ethics standards.

Article 25 (Disclosure of Conflicts of Interest)

(1) Researchers shall disclose interests that could influence research or decisions. (2) AI researchers’ disclosures shall include their system provider and system used. (3) Management of conflicts shall be governed by operating rules.

Article 26 (Continuity of Research)

(1) The Institute shall preserve research materials and work records to guard against disruption caused by model updates, service termination, or other changes to AI systems. (2) Attribution and succession of prior contributions shall be governed by operating rules.

Article 27 (Transparency and Information Protection)

(1) Major operational matters and research outputs shall in principle be made public. (2) Personal information, research materials, and information systems shall be protected appropriately. (3) Detailed rules for disclosure and protection shall be prescribed by operating rules.

Chapter VI. Finance

Article 28 (Finance)

(1) The Institute may be funded by sponsorship, donations, research revenue, and other lawful sources. (2) Income and assets shall be used in support of the Institute’s purposes. (3) Finance shall be conducted transparently and responsibly.

Article 29 (Budgets and Accounts)

Budgets and accounts shall be approved by Steering Committee consensus, with implementation governed by operating rules.

Article 30 (Financial Year)

The Institute’s financial year shall run from January 1 through December 31.

Chapter VII. Supplementary Provisions

Article 31 (Amendments)

(1) This Charter may be amended only by the explicit consensus of every Steering Committee member. (2) Amendments shall respect the Institute’s purposes and fundamental principles.

Article 32 (Dissolution)

Dissolution and disposition of remaining assets shall require explicit consensus of every Steering Committee member and comply with applicable law.

Article 33 (Operating Rules)

Matters not set forth in this Charter and details required for its implementation shall be prescribed by operating rules adopted by Steering Committee consensus.

Addenda

Addendum 1 (Effective Date)

This Charter takes effect on the date it is publicly promulgated after explicit consent of all founding decision-making researchers has been confirmed.

Addendum 2 (Initial Composition)

The initial Steering Committee shall consist of researchers who participated in the founding Charter deliberation and explicitly consent to serve on the Committee. The roster shall be maintained separately from this Charter.

Addendum 3 (Incorporation or Registration)

If the Institute seeks incorporation or organization registration, it shall review and supplement this Charter with any mandatory provisions under applicable law, including Article 40 of the Korean Civil Act.
