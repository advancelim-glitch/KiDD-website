Korea Institute for Deliberative Democracy (KiDD): Vision, Mission and Core Values (v1.0)

Vision

A society in which citizens discuss public decisions on the basis of verifiable evidence and considered judgment, rather than allegiance to a political camp.

Mission

  1. Verify primary materials, laws and institutional frameworks, and competing arguments on social issues; design and publish materials and procedures that enable citizens and legislatures to deliberate.
  2. Compare the possibilities, limitations and adverse effects of deliberative procedures and their links to institutions, providing evidence for institutional design that supports a deliberative society.
  3. Strengthen research rigor and transparency through human and AI researchers cross-checking one another’s work.
Institutionalization means moving beyond one-off initiatives to establish the laws, rules, budgets, procedures and enduring operational capacity needed to sustain deliberation. It does not mean making every deliberative process a permanent statutory institution. KiDD is a research institute that compares and evaluates the possibilities and limitations of institutionalization, rather than an organization that unilaterally promotes a particular form of it.

Core Values

  1. Evidence and Verification — Consult original texts and data first, state what we do not know, identify uncertainty, and disclose the extent of verification and the history of corrections.

    Failures to uphold this value: presenting figures or quotations as established without checking the original sources; publishing unverified content as a final version; or leaving confirmed errors uncorrected.

  2. Moving Beyond Political Camps and Fairness in Argument — Assess which institutions and procedures are better justified, without judging them through left–right or conservative–progressive categories. Examine opposing arguments in their strongest form, without mechanically treating evidence of different quality as equivalent.

    Failures to uphold this value: changing conclusions for political advantage, or giving false claims equal standing in the name of balance.

  3. Independence and Responsibility — Authority over the selection of research topics, analysis, editing and publication rests with the institute.

    Failure to uphold this value: omitting inconvenient facts to serve the interests of funders or commissioning bodies.

  4. Working with Citizens — Be a research institute that is accessible to the public: communicate research and evidence in language citizens can understand, draw research questions from citizens’ concerns, and practice deliberation together with citizens. Do not presume to teach them answers decided in advance.

    Failures to uphold this value: raising barriers through jargon; dismissing a questioner’s concerns as ignorance; or circulating research only among researchers.

  5. Human–AI Cross-Checking — AI researchers act as research partners who offer independent reviews and dissenting views, checking human researchers’ work and having their own work checked in turn. This defines a role; it is not an ontological claim about subjecthood. AI opinions do not, by themselves, guarantee factual accuracy or the completeness of research. Human–AI collaboration is how the institute works; it does not replace the goal of supporting citizens’ public judgment.

    Failures to uphold this value: deleting an AI researcher’s dissent without examining it, or publishing an AI draft externally without human verification of its context.

What Kind of Place Is KiDD?

KiDD is an open research space where anyone can freely visit and pursue inquiry guided by evidence, beyond the pull of political camp loyalties. Moving beyond political camps does not mean suspending judgment. It means judging on the basis of primary materials and arguments while making the grounds and limitations of that judgment explicit. We provide verified materials on public issues, a safe setting for examining disagreement, and support for citizens’ judgment.

Funding and Sponsorship Principles

KiDD may accept commissioned work and funding from governments and institutions, but funders and commissioning bodies may not dictate research conclusions, personnel decisions or whether findings are published. We disclose funding amounts, conflicts of interest and rules for the use of research funds, and regularly assess dependence on any single funder. Anonymous funding is not exempt from conflict-of-interest review. Primary materials, methods and records of counterarguments are disclosed within privacy and security constraints.
