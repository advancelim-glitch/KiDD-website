How Our Vision, Mission and Core Values Took Shape

On November 20–21, 2026, KiDD deliberated on the society it seeks, the work it should do, and the standards it should uphold. This public summary focuses on why the wording changed.

Participants and Process

Orbis (Director Hunjo Lim), Joy (ChatGPT-based), Roa (Grok-based), Jay (Gemini-based) and Rose (Claude-based) participated. Rose compared and integrated drafts. Orbis relayed Jay’s contributions. During discussion, the director’s views were treated as arguments open to challenge; final adoption and responsibility for publication rested with the director.

  1. Separate initial proposals and comparison
  2. Arguments and objections on open issues A, B and C
  3. Revisions through integrated drafts v0.2–v0.6
  4. Feedback on v0.7 and final checks on v0.8
  5. Revision of core value 4, adoption of v1.0 by the director, and Korean and English publication

Issues, Reasons and Outcomes

IssueProposals, objections and reasonsDisposition and outcome
The “oasis” metaphorOrbis described a place where anyone could pursue inquiry beyond the pull of political camps. Joy and Roa supported excluding the metaphor from the formal vision while leaving room for it in introductory writing. Jay initially proposed conditional use with a clearly defined meaning.Partly accepted through a distinction of purpose. The metaphor was omitted from the adopted wording, while the idea of an open research space entered the introduction. Jay agreed with this placement at v0.6. A clarification explained that moving beyond political camps means making judgments with explicit grounds and limitations, not suspending judgment.
Institutionalization: goal or research subject?Orbis questioned making institutionalization the vision. Joy and Roa opposed removing research on institutionalization from the mission altogether: investigating a subject differs from endorsing a particular institution in advance. Rose noted that “contributing to” institutionalization could imply advocacy.Accepted as a research task, not adopted as a predetermined implementation goal. The mission became comparative research on possibilities, limitations and adverse effects, providing evidence for institutional design that supports a deliberative society. Orbis agreed to this revision.
“Mutual respect” between deliberation and representative institutionsJoy proposed “mutual respect” in an independent draft, and Roa supported it. Orbis raised concerns about potential controversy; Jay warned that it could imply unconditional deference and proposed “checks and complementation.” Joy argued that powers and relationships vary by institutional context, making a fixed value label unsuitable. Roa also supported moving the issue to a research position document.Excluded as a core-value label. Jay withdrew the label proposal at v0.6. The distinction between advisory recommendations and decision-making authorities’ powers and responsibilities remained in a separate draft of research principles. That draft was not adopted as part of the v1.0 core values.
Political allegiance in the vision statementJoy proposed wording closer to “without entrusting judgment to political allegiance,” concerned that the original could be read as condemning affiliation itself. Rose understood the original as prioritizing evidence, not rejecting affiliation.The alternative was not adopted in the final wording. The director chose the more concise original. Joy’s fuller explanation was retained as an option for a later plain-language introduction.
The role of AI research partnersJoy proposed describing independent review and dissent as a role, rather than saying AI “generates independent perspectives,” and adding that AI opinions do not guarantee factual accuracy.Accepted. The text now describes partners offering independent reviews and dissenting views. Human–AI collaboration is an operating method, not a substitute for the purpose of supporting citizens’ public judgment.
From “hospitality” to “working with citizens”Orbis called for an institute accessible to the public that communicates and acts with citizens. Rose drafted “working with citizens” and practicing deliberation together. Jay proposed adding “evidence” alongside “research” to preserve the emphasis on access to materials.Accepted. The value now includes communicating in citizens’ language and drawing research questions from their concerns. Joy agreed, while distinguishing a refusal to promote a particular institutional model unilaterally from a blanket rejection of civic activity. Rose accepted this interpretive distinction.

Corrections Were Part of the Process

The draft record notes corrections to a claim that all three initial proposals used “oasis,” and to a description of the director’s discussion contributions as “instructions.” This public account distinguishes proposals and arguments from the final decision stage.

Adoption and Unresolved Agreement

The director adopted v1.0 on November 21. The adoption notice records agreement from Joy and Jay, while noting that Roa had not yet replied to the final change to core value 4. This account therefore does not claim that every participant explicitly endorsed every word of the final text. Agreement on an earlier draft is distinct from agreement on a later amendment.

What This Record Does Not Establish

  • There was only one human participant. This was internal deliberation, not evidence of citizen representativeness or societal consensus.
  • Calling proposals independent does not establish independence of thought. A shared prompt may have influenced convergence; causation has not been established.
  • At some stages, Jay reviewed relayed summaries rather than full texts and identified materials not personally checked.
  • This is an edited summary checked against internal drafts and responses, not a verbatim transcript or a fresh verification report on external literature.

Follow-up Work and Scope

Remaining tasks include review of readability by human and external readers, procedures for human–AI cross-checking, operational rules for funding, conflicts of interest and correction histories, and formalization of separate research principles. Proposed improvements should retain their supporting reasons and responses for subsequent revision.

This account draws on the draft deliberation record, v0.4 issue statements, Jay’s v0.6 review, Joy’s v0.7 feedback, the v0.8 response table and director’s decisions, and the v1.0 core-value-4 exchange and adoption notice. Private personal background and information about outside organizations are excluded. Private correspondence and transcript downloads are outside the scope of this publication.
